Urban expansion and land cover change, India and Hyderabad 

ISRO-NRSC  • May'2025 · published 

•  Developed a dual-resolution framework combining MODIS and
  GLC_FCS30D datasets to map urban expansion across India and
   Hyderabad (2001–2022), enabling land cover change analysis
   and transition matrix generation.

• Quantified cropland loss as the primary source of urban growth
   (63-96% of expansion), reduction in the urban wetlands across
   the major Indian cities.

Workflow

Methodology: Dual-Resolution Urban Expansion Workflow
1. Inputs: MODIS MCD12Q1 land cover (500 m) for 2001 and 2022, and GLC_FCS30D land cover (30 m) for 2001 and 2022.
2. Urban clusters: extract the built-up class from MODIS 2022, group contiguous pixels into discrete clusters using raster connectivity in ArcGIS Pro, and convert each cluster to a polygon with its area. This gave 1,516 clusters.
3. Tier classification: rank clusters by area using percentile thresholds. Tier I (above 218.4 km², top 1.3%) contains 20 clusters, Tier II runs from 18.97 to 218.4 km², and Tier III is below 18.97 km².
4. National land-cover statistics: clip the 2001 and 2022 MODIS rasters to the Tier-I boundaries and compute the area of each land-cover class by zonal statistics.
5. Hyderabad case study: clip MODIS and GLC_FCS30D to the Hyderabad metropolitan region and reclassify GLC_FCS30D to the MODIS IGBP scheme so the two datasets can be compared directly.
6. Transition analysis: merge the 2001 and 2022 raster's with the Combine tool in ArcGIS Pro, record each pixel’s original and final class, and convert the counts to area-based transition matrices.
7. Resolution comparison: apply the same procedures to both datasets and compare what each resolution detects.

Results

National growth: built-up area across the 20 Tier-I clusters rose from 7,592 km² to 8,867 km² (+1,275 km²). New Delhi had the largest gain (+241 km²), while Coimbatore (+27.8%) and Dhanbad (+25.5%) grew fastest in relative terms.
Cropland loss: cropland supplied 63.6% of new urban land nationally (1,141 km²).Hyderabad: 30 m data showed built-up area growing from 660 to 1,036 km² (+57%), with about 96% of the new built-up land taken from cropland.Resolution matters: MODIS captured national trends but understated fragmented peri-urban growth, with only a 12% built-up gain in Hyderabad.
